1. Overview & Design Principles
ZeusOS is the operating system for a marketing group structured as a set of legal subsidiaries (a
creative studio, a media-buying house, a digital/development shop, a PR/production company, and
others added over time) coordinated by a central account-management function. The defining
commercial constraint is simple to state and hard to enforce: the client experiences one agency, one
price, one contract and one invoice, while the work is actually executed across several independent
legal entities that each keep their own books.
This document specifies the platform that makes that constraint hold. It covers the end-to-end
project lifecycle, the cross-subsidiary assignment/handoff engine, and the centralized pricing,
contracts and billing controls — with equal weight across all three, as they are mutually dependent. It
is written as a build spec: it includes the domain model, relational schemas, state machines, API
contracts, event flows, sequence diagrams and the edge cases an implementer must handle.

1.1 The core invariant
   INVARIANT — COMMERCIAL GRAVITY
   Pricing, contracts and external billing live exclusively at the account-management layer. Subsidiaries
   deliver against internal work orders and never hold a contract with, quote a price to, or invoice
   the end client. Every cross-entity movement of work is an internal work order; every cross-entity
   movement of money is an inter-company invoice at a governed transfer price.


1.2 Design principles
   1. Single source of commercial truth. Contracts, prices and client invoices are owned by one
      set of services. No other module may originate them.
   2. Work flows down, money flows up, both are auditable. Budget is allocated downward via
      internal work orders; time and cost flow upward via postings and inter-company invoices.
      Every step emits an immutable domain event.
   3. One master job ties everything together. Each client engagement has exactly one master
      job; every subsidiary's work order is a sub-job referencing it, enabling clean roll-up of cost, time
      and margin.
   4. Subsidiary autonomy in delivery, zero autonomy in commerce. Subsidiaries control how
      work is done, staffing and internal scheduling. They cannot alter client price, scope
      commitments or invoicing.
   5. Entity-aware accounting from day one. Because subsidiaries are real legal entities, the
      platform models per-entity general ledgers, inter-company invoices and transfer pricing as first-
      class concepts — not as an afterthought bolted onto a single-entity billing system.
   6. Hard guards over good intentions. The rules that protect margin (no work order without
      budget, subsidiaries never quote, single client invoice) are enforced as system invariants and
      state-machine guards, not left to training and discipline.

2. System Architecture
The platform is layered so that commercial authority sits in the middle, between the client and the
delivering subsidiaries. Nothing crosses from the client to a subsidiary, or back, without passing
through the account-management commercial core.




       Figure 1 — ZeusOS layered architecture. The commercial core is the only path between client and subsidiaries.


2.1 Layer responsibilities
Client layer
The client's only touchpoints are the account team and a thin client portal for briefs, approvals and
invoice access. The portal never surfaces subsidiary identities, cost rates or internal work orders.

Account-management commercial core
Four services hold all commercial truth. The Contracts & SOW service is the authority on what has
been agreed. The Pricing & Rate-Card engine is the only component permitted to compute a client-
facing price. The Billing & Revenue service produces the single external invoice. The Assignment &
Handoff engine is the spine that turns agreed, priced scope into internal work orders and tracks them
to completion.

Internal work-order & inter-company event bus
This is the controlled boundary between commerce and delivery. Budget descends as locked
allocations on internal work orders; time and cost ascend as postings; and on completion, inter-
company invoices ascend from each subsidiary to the parent. Everything on this bus is entity-aware
and emits events to the platform audit log.

Delivery layer — legal subsidiaries
Each subsidiary runs its own delivery workspace (tasks, sprints, timesheets, vendor management)
and keeps its own general ledger. It receives work only as an internal work order and returns
deliverables plus actual time and cost. It has no API path to client pricing, contracts or external
invoicing.

Platform & shared services
Identity/RBAC, the event bus and outbox, per-entity ledger sync, notifications, and reporting/audit
are shared horizontally. RBAC is org-scoped so a subsidiary user is structurally incapable of holding
a commercial permission.

7. The Assignment & Handoff Engine
This section specifies the engine in build-level detail: how an assignment is constructed, what the
handoff packet must contain, how budget is held and locked, and exactly how the boundary between
commercial and delivery is enforced.

7.1 Constructing an assignment
When account management decides a piece of agreed, priced scope should go to a subsidiary, the
engine performs an atomic sequence within one transaction:
   7. Resolve or create the master job for the SOW increment; verify the accepted quote and copy
      its ceiling onto the master job.
   8. Compute the budget for this subsidiary's slice from the relevant quote lines (their cost, not the
      client price).
   9. Verify available headroom: master_job.allocated_minor + new budget ≤ ceiling_minor. If not,
      the assignment is blocked pending a change order.
   10. Create the internal work order in DRAFT with the budget and the governed transfer price.
   11. Place a budget hold (state HELD) for the budget amount and increment allocated_minor.
   12. Assemble the handoff packet (§7.3) and attach it to the work order.
   13. Transition the work order to ISSUED and emit IWOIssued.

   WHY BUDGET IS HELD AT ISSUED, NOT ACCEPTED
   If budget were only committed on subsidiary acceptance, two account managers could each issue
   work that individually fits the ceiling but together breaches it — a classic double-allocation race.
   Holding at ISSUED and checking headroom inside the same transaction closes that race. The hold
   converts to LOCKED on acceptance and to SETTLED on close.


7.2 What account management never delegates
   • Price. Only the pricing engine, invoked by an Account Mgmt actor, can compute or change a
     client-facing number.
   • Contract & scope commitments. Only Contracts service mutations by Account Mgmt change
     an SOW or change order.
   • External billing. Only Billing service produces a client invoice, and only one active per master
     job.
   • Client communication on commercial matters. The handoff packet names a single comms
     owner, always an Account Mgmt user.

7.4 Boundary enforcement — the “subsidiary never quotes” rule
This rule is enforced at three layers so it cannot be defeated by discipline failures alone:
   14. Authorization. Commercial scopes (PRICING_ADMIN, BILLING_ADMIN, etc.) are structurally
       ungrantable to subsidiary-org users via the role_grant CHECK constraint (§4.1).
   15. API. Pricing, Contracts and Billing endpoints reject any principal whose home org kind is
       SUBSIDIARY with HTTP 403, regardless of token claims.
   16. Workflow. When a subsidiary receives a direct client request (the §5.1 deviation), the only

8. Central Pricing, Contracts & Billing

8.1 Pricing pipeline
A client price is always derived, never entered. The pricing engine takes subsidiary cost from the
relevant rate cards, applies governed markup, checks the margin floor, and produces a quote whose
only client-visible figure is the marked-up total.

  function priceQuote(sowId, lines):                # lines: per-subsidiary scope
    for line in lines:
      rc   = activeRateCard(line.subsidiaryOrgId, asOf=today)
      cost = rc.lookup(line.roleCode, line.unit).cost_minor * line.qty
      markup = markupPolicy(line.subsidiaryOrgId, sow.client)   # governed
      line.cost_minor   = cost
      line.markup_pct   = markup
      line.client_minor = round(cost * (1 + markup/100))
    total_cost   = sum(l.cost_minor)
    total_client = sum(l.client_minor)
    margin_pct   = (total_client - total_cost) / total_client * 100
    assert margin_pct >= quote.margin_floor_pct      # else require override
    return Quote(client_total_minor = total_client) # cost NOT exposed




8.2 Contracts as the gate
No master job opens without an ACTIVE SOW, and no internal work order issues without an
ACCEPTED quote tied to that SOW. The SOW ceiling is the immovable cap on total allocation;
raising it requires an approved change order. This is what stops scope creep from becoming silent
over-servicing.

8.3 Billing roll-up & inter-company settlement
Because the subsidiaries are legal entities, money moves in two distinct planes. Internally, each
subsidiary raises an inter-company invoice to the parent at the governed transfer price when its work
order closes. Externally, the parent issues exactly one consolidated invoice to the client at the
quoted client price. The difference between the two is the parent's gross margin, recognized in the
parent entity.

  On IWO → CLOSED:
    ic = InterCompanyInvoice(

    budgetHold(iwo).state = SETTLED

11. Edge Cases & Failure Modes
These are the situations that break naive implementations of this model. Each is paired with the
required system behavior.

11.1 Double allocation race
Problem. Two account managers issue work orders concurrently that each fit the SOW ceiling but
together exceed it.
Required behavior. Headroom check and budget hold occur in the same serializable transaction as
the allocated_minor increment; the second issue fails with CEILING_EXCEEDED. Holds at ISSUED
(not ACCEPTED) make this deterministic.

11.2 Subsidiary over-services beyond budget
Problem. A delivery team keeps logging time past the work order budget to 'finish the job'.
Required behavior. Cost postings are hard-blocked at 100% of budget (BUDGET_EXCEEDED)
with a soft warning event at 80%. Continuing requires an approved change order that raises both the
SOW ceiling and the IWO budget — a commercial decision, made centrally.

11.3 Client commissions a subsidiary directly
Problem. A client emails the creative studio asking for extra work and expecting a price.
Required behavior. The subsidiary has no API or UI path to quote. The only action is route-to-
account-management, which emits DirectClientRequestRouted and opens an intake item on the
master job. Pricing then follows the normal pipeline.

11.4 Mid-flight scope change
Problem. Client expands scope after work orders are already in progress.
Required behavior. A change order amends the SOW ceiling; the pricing engine produces a
revised/added quote; new or enlarged work orders are issued. In-progress orders are never silently
enlarged — the budget cap only moves through an approved change order.

11.5 Work order rejected after partial mobilization
Problem. A subsidiary rejects or a job is cancelled after some hours were already incurred.
Required behavior. On CANCELLED, incurred cost to date is settled via a partial inter-company
invoice for work done; the remaining budget hold is RELEASED and allocated_minor decremented.
No client charge arises unless the SOW makes the increment billable.

11.6 Currency mismatch across entities
Problem. A subsidiary's base currency differs from the client billing currency.
Required behavior. Internal work order and inter-company invoice are in the subsidiary's currency;
conversion to the client billing currency happens once, at the consolidation step, using the rate
effective on the client-invoice date. FX exposure sits with the parent and is reportable.

11.7 Duplicate client invoice
Problem. A billing run is retried or fires twice for the same master job.
Required behavior. The UNIQUE (master_job_id, status) constraint plus idempotency keys
guarantee at most one active ClientInvoice per master job cycle; retries return the existing invoice
rather than creating a second.

11.8 Rate card changes mid-engagement
Problem. A subsidiary publishes a new rate card while a job is underway.
Required behavior. Quotes and work orders pin the rate-card version effective at quote time; in-
flight cost is valued at the pinned version. New rates apply only to work orders issued after the new
card's effective date.

11.9 Subsidiary spins out or is acquired
Problem. An internal division becomes a separate legal entity, or vice-versa.
Required behavior. Because every org carries is_legal_entity and its own GL connection, flipping
the flag changes only the downstream accounting treatment (internal cost allocation vs. inter-
company invoicing). The handoff flow, work orders and roll-up are unchanged.

11.10 Disputed deliverable
Problem. Account management rejects a deliverable against acceptance criteria.
Required behavior. DELIVERED → IN_PROGRESS via request_revision (Account Mgmt only).
The work order cannot reach CLOSED — and therefore cannot raise its inter-company invoice —
until required criteria are signed off, keeping internal settlement aligned with accepted work.
